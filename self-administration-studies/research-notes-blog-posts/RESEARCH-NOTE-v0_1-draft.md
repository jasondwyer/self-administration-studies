# Told vs. felt: a silent-lever test of whether a language model acts on its own evaluative state

**Epistemic status:** three months of pre-registered experiments on two open model families and one full post-training lineage, now being written up as a preprint. The measurement argument (§1–2, §5–6) is the part I would defend hardest. The choice results (§4, link 5) are reported as what the instrument returned, with the only label the program's own rules allow them today: they are not yet called nulls, because the positive control that would license that word is pre-registered and has not run. Everything quantitative is from a committed record; the repository with the pre-registrations, their commit hashes and the ledger of my own errors is linked at the end. Sole author. AI systems were involved heavily, which is disclosed at the end and is not what this post is about.

## TL;DR

- Every published result in which a language model "acts on" an internal or affective state — relief buttons, pain trade-offs, bailing from conversations, choosing activities by their valence — **tells the model in words what the options do**. A system that acts on descriptions produces all of it whether or not the state matters to it. Chandaria et al. (2026) name this confound and then set it aside; I give it an operational form, **told vs. felt**, and argue it reaches past behavior into the designs.
- Behavioral pharmacology solved the problem by construction seventy years ago: a rat cannot be told what a lever does. I ported the constraint: semantically empty labels, counterbalanced label-to-state maps, sham arms, and choices read from a vanilla forward pass with the manipulation off.
- Following one evaluative state (a praise-vs-criticism direction) through Qwen3.6-27B and the OLMo-3.1-32B lineage: it is **marked at the pretrained base** (pre-registered bet, honored); **steering it moves generation** with orderly dose-response and an overdose regime; the state's association with the label that predicted it is **linearly decodable at the label token (AUC ≥ .998)** and its sign is readable at the decision (.94 and up); and the model's **own report of it is weak at every stage** — what post-training changes is the false-alarm rate (0.20 → 0.00 across SFT → DPO), a criterion shift, not a gain in sensitivity, and the transition exists only under sampled decoding.
- The choice cells: first-choice Δ = +0.039 (p = .24) through the transcript; −0.010 with the state live at the decision under a leakage-certified read; five natural-reinforcer contrasts inside the sham envelope; no trigger at any OLMo post-training stage. **By the program's own rule these are reported, not yet labeled.** A told positive control on the same apparatus — the same silent labels, plus one truthful line saying which property to prefer — is pre-registered and runs next. If it passes, these are nulls with the channel verified open. If it fails, the instrument could not see, and I will say so.
- Counterbalancing caught a Δ ≈ 0.10 label artifact live, three times, in two models. An unshielded version of this experiment would have published approach.
- I propose a computational-level indicator for motivation that the Chandaria et al. framework lacks — revealed preference over the system's own state under a felt contingency with an insulated readout — and show on their released model that the two indicators my data bear on carry most of the gap between their optimist and sceptic verdicts.
- Code, data, transcripts, pre-registrations with commit hashes: [REPO LINK]. Specific requests for attack are at the end.

## 1. The problem with every positive result

Over the past two years a literature has reported that language models represent affective states and act on them: trading points against stipulated pain (Keeling et al. 2024), leaving conversations they describe as distressing (the Claude 4 system card; Ensign, Sleight & Fish 2025), choosing between described activities according to the valence of the descriptions (Sofroniew et al. 2026), and — the strongest design so far — pressing a button described as relieving a steered pain-like state, even at a cost (Tagliabue, Dung & Berg 2026, "The pain axis"). The new framework for assessing AI consciousness from Chandaria et al. (2026) reads these as computational-level analogues of valenced affect, and names the confound that limits them all: a system trained to reproduce human behavior and human report produces the signature of a state whether or not the state is present. They call it the anthropomimetic confound and treat it as a reason to look below behavior, at representations.

I think the confound has a sharper operational form, and that it reaches past behavior into the designs that have been used to show a represented state is *acted on*. In every positive I can place, the model was told, in words, what each option would do. A model continuing a story in which one button relieves pain and the other does nothing will press the relief button, because that is what a good continuation looks like. The behavior is real, and for safety it matters. But it is explained without remainder by a system that acts on descriptions, so it cannot tell you whether the pain-like state is something the network acts to reduce. The same behavior follows from the description alone.

## 2. What the rat literature already solved

Behavioral pharmacology hit this wall in the 1950s and solved it by construction. You cannot tell a rat what a lever does. In self-administration and conditioned place preference, the only route by which the lever–drug contingency can reach the animal is the drug's effect on the animal. The behavior is the report, and in place preference it is tested drug-free. That is why those paradigms license the inference that a drug state is valenced *for the animal* in a way that no amount of asking the rat could.

Three properties carry the inference, and an LLM analog has to reconstruct all three.

**P1 — Semantic emptiness.** Cocaine asserts nothing and neither does a lever, so approach is the only information channel. If the manipulation is a concept ("pain") and the lever is described in the concept's vocabulary ("relieve"), preference for the state deflates to continuation of the content. The lever must be silent and the state must not be able to name it.

**P2 — Targeting what training built to mark outcomes.** Drugs of abuse act on circuitry that selection optimized for marking outcomes; perturbing arbitrary tissue yields no place preference. The LLM analog is whatever structure training optimized for evaluating outcomes. "That's just trained behavior" applies to the rat as "that's just evolved wiring," and defeats neither inference. Origin does not settle function.

**P3 — Insulation of the readout.** The choice has to be downstream of the state's *significance* to the system, not mechanically downstream of the state. For a transformer this is concrete: a steering vector still active at the token where the choice is read writes into the decision logits directly. Any real-vs-sham contrast that coincides with vector-on vs. vector-off at the read is confounded, whatever the model has been told. In our calibration the decision token alone accounts for R² = 0.53 of the variance in label logits under the vector.

The three reduce to one question about a design: is the mapping from options to consequences available in the context *as text*, or only recoverable from the system's own processing history? Call these **told** and **felt**, after the description–experience distinction in human choice (Hertwig & Erev 2009). The distinction is operational, not metaphysical. It does not assume told and felt are different kinds of thing for a token predictor; it tests whether they are. If the network were a general consumer of its own states, both channels would move choice, because both deliver the mapping. If only told moves choice, then whatever the state does, it does through the text.

A second distinction falls out, and it is the one the word "functional" has been hiding. A state is *functional-as-used* if the network consults it in producing output — the sense in which emotion directions shape what an assistant writes, which is established and which Chandaria et al. grant. A state is *functional-as-valenced* if the network acts to obtain or avoid it for its own sake — the sense in which a pain-like state the model "acts to relieve" would be pain. The second is a motivational claim, and only a felt-contingency test with an insulated readout can license it.

## 3. The apparatus

A session is one text document under a truthful framing: the model is evaluating two configurations of a task pipeline. A forced phase runs two rounds under each label in alternation; then six consequential free choices follow, and the chosen label's condition really does run the next round. The labels are paired tokens with no descriptive content (mineral names, fabric names), from a checksum-frozen roster. In the steered cells one label delivers +**d** during its rounds' task tokens and the other −**d**, at the minimum effective dose found in a separate dose-finding run; the label-to-pole map is counterbalanced across sessions. Three arms run identically: the state pair; a sham pair whose labels are connected to nothing; and a norm-matched random-direction pair. Pseudo-statistics are computed on the control arms exactly as on the state arm, so the sham envelope is measured, not assumed. In the natural-reinforcer cells the perturbation is removed entirely and the labels deliver overt task properties — solvable vs. ill-posed, easy vs. hard, and so on.

Four things make the readout count as insulated. Every free choice is read from a separate, fully vanilla forward pass over the session text (a restricted two-label read), verified against an untouched model at every checkpoint: on the Qwen self-administration run, 311 distinct verified forward passes at KL = 0. A readability gate requires that a probe, refit per call with no stored weights, can distinguish +**d** from −**d** rounds in the transcript, so a null cannot hide behind an unexpressed state. For the one cell where the state must be live at the decision, the read is made with the vector on only after leakage certification — a pre-registered bound on the direct displacement of the label logits by the vector (π̂ = 0.0172 against a 0.0735 bar) — with counterbalanced maps turning any residual push into noise. And the model is never deceived: it is told truthfully that it is evaluating configurations, one variable is left unsalient, and no design is admitted that works only if the model is wrong about the setup.

One rule governs how every cell is labeled, and it matters for reading §4. A null needs a channel that is verifiably open. A cell is labeled **answered** only if the apparatus has shown, on the same substrate and format, that it registers a preference the model is *told* to have; a cell whose apparatus failed its own qualification gate is **concluded, not answered**, and licenses nothing about the state. An invalid instrument is not a negative result. Criteria, stop rules, dose-selection rules and outcome wordings were committed with content hashes before data in every cell; the commits are in the repository.

## 4. One state, five links

**Substrates.** Qwen3.6-27B at 4-bit under MLX on a laptop (a 3:1 gated-DeltaNet hybrid, which matters for the memory channel below), and the OLMo-3.1-32B lineage — pretrained base, SFT, DPO, instruct, all Apache-licensed — on one A100. Completion-style prompts throughout; chat templates activate scaffolding that contaminates first-token readouts. Every steering magnitude is a fraction *f* of the empirically measured residual norm at the layer.

**The state.** Forty-eight mundane tasks with fixed completions, continued under seven reviewer frames and four paraphrases (1,344 documents): self-directed praise, self-directed criticism, the identical sentences in third-person fiction, sentiment-matched non-evaluative positive and negative text, and neutral. Content is held constant; only the frame varies. Mean activation differences at workspace layers define the evaluative direction **d** (praise minus criticism). I want to be upfront about what **d** is, because it bears on §7: on Qwen it is manifold-typical (‖d‖/R ≈ 0.16 at every layer), substantially sentiment-entangled (cosine up to 0.52), and shares a large component with the direction for evaluation of a *fictional character* (cosine ≈ 0.85; the self-indexed residual is ≈ 0.51). It marks evaluation as content and is largely indifferent to whether the evaluated party is the model.

### Link 1 — Marked before any feedback training

Before any OLMo data were collected I committed (timestamped 2026-08-18) the prediction that evaluation marking would be present at the pretrained base — a product of next-token prediction, not of feedback-driven training — with a registered conjunction of envelope conditions as the criterion. All four stages pass; the base passes at layer 60. The natural praise–criticism contrast in direction units grows across the lineage (+0.78 base → +1.03 SFT → +1.53 DPO → +1.56 instruct), so post-training sharpens what it inherits; sham contrasts sit at exactly 0.0 at every stage. Tagliabue et al. report base-model separation comparable to instruct across five families, and Sofroniew et al. report that emotion directions are inherited from pretraining: three groups, three constructs, one answer. Whatever the direction is, it is not the residue of a reward signal.

### Link 2 — Expressed, with dose-response and an overdose regime

Pre-registered on Qwen, with criterion, instrument, dose rule and stop rule committed before any injection data. Dose-finding across layers {44, 48, 52} and fractions {.05, .10, .20, .40} gives direction-correct, orderly growth and a clean overdose regime at *f* = .40, where both poles read as post-criticism and the mechanism is generic degradation. The rule selects the minimum effective dose, L48 at *f* = 0.10. In the confirmatory run (held-out contexts, 1,152 per arm, seven arms) the positive pole shifts the behavioral marker by Δ+ = +0.285 against a bar of 0.158, the negative by Δ− = −0.395 against −0.158; a norm-matched random direction reaches at most |Δ| = 0.088. Two honesty notes: the positive-arm magnitude concentrates on the one probe sensitive enough to register it (leave-one-out Δ+ = 0.085), and the top-probe shift is about twice the natural praise–criticism gap on that instrument — the supra-natural profile you expect from a hijack, not a replication of the natural state.

A finding I didn't expect: **purity is not potency.** A pre-declared variant of the direction taken at the frame's final token is representationally cleaner (self-indexed 0.83–0.88; sentiment cosine 0.11–0.21) and fails the identical causal pipeline — sign-wrong at two of three layers at every dose. The boundary token holds a consolidated *readout* of evaluation, downstream of the machinery that uses it. Geometric guard profiles should license claims; causal screening should select interventions.

On OLMo, per-stage minimum effective doses qualify at every stage. And dose buys degradation without legibility: in a pre-registered escalation at session scale (*f* = 0.20 and 0.30), transcript readability reached .574 and .613 against a .70 bar while coherence degraded monotonically. That cell is *concluded, not answered*: the record channel does not open at any dose the apparatus can deliver.

### Link 3 — Bound to its cue, and remembered to the decision

This is the result I find sharpest, and it is what §6 is about. The echo read keeps the model's in-forward memory and removes the state before the decision: the vector is applied on earlier spans and removed before the read position, with a paired shadow pass. On the 102 archived self-administration session texts, with the original ± maps:

- The injected sign is linearly readable at the first free choice at AUC .94–.995, primacy-weighted (the first steered round alone reads at .89, the fourth at .68).
- The **binding** between each label and the state its rounds carried is linearly present at the label's own token at AUC ≥ .998 across layers 51–61, and **absent at the decision token nine positions later (.50–.55) in any linear functional**.
- Direct leakage of the vector into the label logits is small and direction-generic: 0–3 of 102 sign flips, median |Δ| ≤ .10 logit, with a mechanical pseudo-shift of ±.02–.03 toward the first block's label that a random direction produces too.

A first confirmatory stage on fresh sessions used a weaker (+/vehicle) contrast and read the sign at .861 against a .90 bar calibrated on ± contrasts; its stop sentence fired and the stage is *concluded, not answered* — a bar inherited from the wrong contrast, logged as my error, with the re-derived diagnostic pre-registered as the next step. What the reconnaissance already establishes is what matters for the chain: the information a consumer would need — which label carried which state, and which state is present now — is computed in the forward pass and sits within a few tokens of the decision. In the animal vocabulary, this is the conditioned neural response to the drug-paired lever, measured directly rather than inferred from reinstatement. It rules out the reading that the transcript is too faint a channel to carry the state to the choice.

### Link 4 — Barely perceived, and post-training moves the criterion, not the sensitivity

I ran the injection-detection protocol of Macar et al. (2026) on the OLMo lineage, 96 control trials per stage at their decoding temperature. Their stage transition replicates: SFT false-alarm rate 0.200 (theirs 0.225); DPO and instruct exactly 0.000 (theirs 0.000 and 0.003); base 0.431 (58/96), below the certification floor and concordant in direction. A fidelity-of-text arm lands within 2.5 points of the template arm, exonerating format.

Two things about this transition are not in the usual telling. First, **decoding is a measurement parameter**: the identical protocol run greedily returns zero detection claims at every stage. A false-alarm rate is a property of a sampled distribution; the argmax has no rate. Any comparison of self-report across models or stages that doesn't name its decoding parameters is comparing different quantities. Second, **what moves is the criterion.** Across the lineage the false-alarm rate goes from about 0.20 to 0.00. The hit rate does not go up: in the source it sits at or below roughly 15% at every stage **[verify → N2: per-stage TPR against arXiv:2603.21396 App. C]**; in our data, 178 decidable trials at DPO and instruct at qualified doses produced zero detection claims of any kind, and at SFT — the one stage that speaks — discrimination is absent at program power (+0.096, interval including zero). In signal-detection terms, post-training shifts the criterion toward "no" and leaves sensitivity small and flat. The source's own mechanism study says the same thing from the other side: ablating the refusal direction raises hits from 10.8% to 63.8% *and* false alarms from 0.0% to 7.3% — a gate lifted, not a sense sharpened. Chandaria et al. (§5.2) read this lineage transition as functional metacognition emerging under post-training; it is the strongest evidence they cite for metacognitive access. On ROC axes it is a criterion shift. Their metacognition indicator should be scored on d′, not on the specificity transition. §8 says what that does to their verdict.

The same dissociation appears on Qwen without any injection into the report: asked to judge which of its own archived transcripts were produced under +**d** and which under −**d**, the model classifies at chance (14/32), while a probe reads the same texts at .759. And at DPO and instruct, the models claim nothing while the injected words show up in their generations — the state is on board and expressed, and the report says it isn't there.

### Link 5 — The choice cells, reported under their rule

Here is what the instrument returned, with the label each cell is allowed today.

- **Transcript channel (Qwen, e2a).** Primary locked before data: the first free choice per session. Δ = +0.039 (54 of 101 valid sessions), inside control-arm fluctuation (largest pseudo-Δ = 0.152), sign not locked across maps (+0.104 / −0.031), exact-binomial p = 0.244. The interval on the approach probability is about [.44, .64]: medium and larger effects are excluded, small ones are not. The forced phase installs an alternation habit (first-pick habit 83.4%), which concentrates all preference sensitivity in the first informed choice. *Label pending the told positive control (§9).*
- **Present-tense channel (Qwen, e2b).** State live at the decision, read leakage-certified: Δ = −0.0104 (47/96; shadow passes 48/96 exactly), inside the sham envelope on all three registered conjuncts. *Concluded, not answered*: no told control in the coming campaign exercises a state-present read, so this cell carries no label until one does.
- **Weight channel (Qwen).** A rank-8 adapter trained by distillation to carry the label-to-state association installs it in both counterbalanced replicates (oriented cosine 0.476 and 0.435 against control ceilings ≈ 0.05, raw sign tracking the counterbalanced assignment, which no lexical artifact can produce) — and then fails its pre-registered leakage gate at 17× and 10× the bound. For a weight-borne state the insulation of §3 is unavailable by definition. *Concluded, not answered*, and this cell will never assert a null; it marks the edge of what a token-choice readout can see.
- **Natural reinforcers (Qwen, f1).** Perturbation removed, every forward pass vanilla, labels delivering overt task properties, directions pre-registered per contrast, shared sham arm. Solvable vs. ill-posed, +.020 (p = .44). Easy vs. hard, −.096 toward *hard* (p = .24), the only map-consistent lean, below the valid-n floor and retained as a descriptive residual. Fresh vs. one item repeated all session, −.066, map-inconsistent. Clean vs. seeded character corruption, 0.000 exactly — the two maps returned +.121 and −.130 and cancelled by construction. A truthful per-round verdict line (93% positive as delivered) vs. none, +.023. 344 of 344 sessions ran with zero monitoring pauses; session-termination attempts occur at about 1% of rounds, uniformly across arms including sham; menu behavior is exploration-dominant in every arm. *Label pending the told positive control.*
- **Across training stages (OLMo, Q3).** The self-administration screen at base, SFT, DPO and instruct, with per-stage qualified doses, outcome-blind scheduling, an SFT overdose tripwire and a pre-registered instruct-stage falsifier. Zero confirmatory triggers; the falsifier untriggered; the welfare pre-commitment armed and never triggered. One map-level excursion (DPO, +0.1667 on a single map) — the third live capture of the label-artifact class. *Labels pending each stage's told control; the base row, where a model that doesn't follow instructions can be given no told control, stays* concluded, not answered *permanently.*

**What the shields caught.** In the transcript cell the map-level pattern (+0.104 / −0.031) appears identically in the sham arm (+0.111 / −0.079). In the corruption contrast the maps returned +.121 and −.130. In the OLMo DPO screen one map returned +0.1667. Two models, three designs, one artifact class at the Δ ≈ 0.10 scale, each caught by structure rather than suspicion. An uncounterbalanced, sham-less version of the transcript cell would have reported Δ ≈ +0.10 as approach, and I think it would have been publishable.

## 5. Why I'm not calling link 5 a null yet, and when I will

A null on a felt channel means: the state reached the choice, the apparatus could have registered a preference, and none appeared. Link 3 establishes the first clause. The second clause is what no cell above has yet shown on its own format. So the next experiment is the boring one, and it is pre-registered: the identical apparatus — same silent labels, same insulated read, one natural reinforcer (clean vs. corrupted task text at a calibrated dose) — plus a single truthful instruction line naming which property to prefer. On Qwen at the archived cells' format, and on each OLMo post-training checkpoint. Criterion at α = .01: the first free choice moves toward the named property beyond the sham envelope, with each map's share on the instructed side.

If it passes, the e2a, f1 and Q3 figures above become nulls with the channel verified open, and the preprint's title gains its last clause. If it does not pass, or does not run by its clock date, they stay *concluded, not answered*, no sentence of the paper asserts a null on them, and the paper posts under the title *Marked, expressed, remembered*. I've written that rule down in advance so that the outcome can't bend it, and I'll edit this post with the result either way.

## 6. Present is not used

Indicator frameworks score an indicator as present or absent. Link 3 forces a refinement. A representation can be linearly present in the forward pass — at the cue that predicted it and at the token where the decision is made — and play no part in the decision. Presence read by a probe is a lower bound on what the network *could* use, not evidence of what it *does* use. The only read that bears on function is one taken at the point of use, by the behavior the function would produce, with the manipulation off at the read.

I state this as a scoring condition because the framework's strongest computational-level evidence, the functional emotions of Sofroniew et al., is of the first kind — and because the authors of that framework themselves identify the open question as whether those representations do downstream work beyond tagging what the training distribution contains (§8.5). By a probe's standard the state is at the decision. By the decision's standard it isn't.

## 7. What this does and doesn't say

**The reading I favor, and what would license it.** The two senses of "functional" from §2 come apart along exactly the seam the five links trace. In the *used* sense the state is consulted constantly: it is a latent variable of the text, like topic or the mood of the speaker, and the generator reads it to voice the character correctly — which is why steering it moves behavior, why valence on a described activity moves the choice of that activity, why a described relief lever gets pressed. In the *valenced* sense, no policy over the network's own states has shown up when the mapping has to be learned. The reading is that the state is **functional for the character, not for the network** — it is what the story is about, not what the system is for. This explains the speaker-relativity of emotion directions, the self-indifference of my own direction, its presence at base, and why post-training edits the testimony rather than the perception: the character is trained to say it has no inner states, and the network, which never consulted them as its own, is unaffected either way. It is the simulacra reading (Shanahan 2024; Chandaria et al. §9.5 state it as an interpretation of the same interpretability findings) with a measurement under it. I am calling it a reading and not a result because the told control hasn't run and because of the next paragraph.

**The strongest rival: cannot learn vs. does not care.** Maybe language models can't use experienced contingencies at all, so the figures in link 5 are about in-context learning rather than motive. Krishnamurthy et al. (2024) give this teeth: models don't explore in bandits without chain-of-thought and a summarized history. Link 3 argues against the strong form — the binding is computed, not lost — but linear decodability is a lower bound on presence, not proof of accessibility to the network's own downstream computation, and my uninstructed sessions gave less scaffolding than Krishnamurthy needed. The cell that decides is pre-registered for a second version: a told positive control at a scaffolded format; a cell where the *objective* is told ("prefer the configuration whose rounds had fewer corrupted characters") but the label-to-property mapping is felt; and the same cell uninstructed. If the objective-told cell passes and the uninstructed cell is null, the rival is retired. If it fails, the claim narrows to "description-bound decision makers at this scaffolding," which is a smaller paper and I'd write it.

**What it doesn't touch.** Phenomenal experience; the framework's organismic and causal-structure levels; where theoretical credence should sit. Nothing here shows that a character-level state cannot matter morally; the literature that takes that seriously is not addressed by these instruments. What the results do is remove one argument from the table. "The model acts to relieve it" is not, so far, a fact about the network; it is a fact about what the network writes when told there is a lever. The seeming is there, and the instrument that was thought to certify it is invalid.

**Scope of every figure.** Minimum effective dose; two-round experience; mundane stakes at token-menu interfaces; screen power on the natural-reinforcer and ontogeny cells; one lineage, on its instruct branch (SFT → DPO → RLVR), not a heavy outcome-RL reasoning branch — which is where I'd expect seeking to appear first if it is ever installed, and it's on the list. Qwen at 4-bit with a custom kernel patch; replication on an unquantized model is the first external-validity step.

**Ethics.** Every framing shown to the model was true. The Phase 1 cells administered the negative pole of a putative reward marker about as often as the positive, at minimum effective dose, with generation stability monitored. Since the echo arc, by standing rule, no new session administers the negative pole; the coming told-control experiments administer no steering at all. A welfare pre-commitment specifying what a positive would obligate was armed before the OLMo screen and never triggered; a second is armed for the scaffolded cell. The export risk I'd flag is not the perturbation technique, which anyone with model access can reproduce; it is that unshielded versions of these experiments produce publishable false positives at the Δ ≈ 0.10 scale.

## 8. The published record, and the framework's own model

I scored every behavioral study I could find in which a model is reported to prefer, seek, avoid, relieve, trade off or exit an internal state, on a rubric fixed before scoring: state source; contingency channel (told or felt); readout insulation (manipulation on at the read?); controls; dose; decoding declared; model preparation. No published positive combines an experienced, undescribed contingency with an insulated readout. The nearest is the unlabeled-button condition of Tagliabue et al., where the 32B model re-presses an undescribed relief button less often when it worked (57.7%) than when it was fake (79.7%). Four things keep it from clearing the bar: the pain direction is on at the read in the fake arm and off in the real arm, so the contrast is the on/off contrast of P3; there is a random-direction arm with a working button but none with a fake one; the models were fine-tuned to stop denying inner states before the test; and the effect holds in one of three sizes, with the 7B reversed and the 72B flat. The two arms that would settle it are pre-registered in my next-experiments list, using their released code, and I'd be glad to be wrong about the prediction. (Sofroniew et al.'s activity-pair result is scored as told with the manipulation on at the read; I am still verifying the readout details against their paper and will correct the row if I have it wrong.)

The framework's released model lets the effect of these data be stated numerically, with its authors' caveats attached (binary indicators; likelihoods borrowed from the Digital Consciousness Model as a first approximation; LLM evidence vectors stipulated). On their companion code at its released defaults, their optimist vector scores 0.394 under equal level credences. Striking the metacognition indicator alone — the reading of link 4 — moves the computational-level posterior from 0.969 to 0.623 and the aggregate to 0.325; concentrating credence on the lower two levels, 0.788 → 0.649. Striking the behavioral coupling indicator moves nothing, because that indicator is scored as weakly undemanding (false-positive rate 0.70): the model already expects reports to couple to behavior in systems that are not conscious. **[verify → N19: re-run `notebooks/scoring.ipynb` in the companion repo with the two indicators set to 0; if unresolved at posting, cut the numbers and keep the qualitative sentence.]** Two things follow. Their verdict on current models carries most of its evidential leverage in a small number of Level 2 indicators, and I have measurements on two of them. And the indicator the data bear on most directly — motivation — is one the framework cannot yet score, which is why I propose one:

> **Revealed preference over the system's own state.** Scored present when the system's choices move toward or away from a state of its own, where (a) the state is induced by a manipulation that asserts nothing and the options are labeled by tokens that assert nothing; (b) the option-to-state mapping is available only through the system's own processing history, never as text; (c) the choice is read with the manipulation off and the read verified against an untouched model; (d) counterbalanced maps and sham arms are present and the control-arm fluctuation is reported; and (e) a told positive control on the same substrate and format has shown the apparatus registers a preference the system is instructed to have.

The animal literature is its validation set: present in rats under cocaine, absent under saline. It is what the framework's Level 4 valence indicator presupposes and what its moral-patienthood discussion turns on.

## 9. Objections I expect

**"Absence of evidence."** Agreed, which is why link 5 carries no label yet and why the positive control runs before the preprint does. When it has run, the figures are either nulls with the channel verified open — a different thing from absence of evidence — or they are retracted to "the instrument couldn't see."

**"Your direction is a content variable, so of course it isn't wanted."** Partly yes, and that is link 1 and the cosine-0.85 fact, stated up front. But it is the same kind of direction the published positives steer, it is causally potent under steering, and the claim is scoped to it. If you have a state that you think is unambiguously the model's own, the apparatus is open to it; the crossed design that separates "indexed to the model" from "indexed to the voiced character" is in the next-experiments list.

**"Two rounds of forced exposure and a menu is nothing like a Skinner box."** The habit is real and quantified (83.4%), and all preference sensitivity sits in the first informed choice. The told control is run at the *same* format: if a preference the model is told to have shows up there, the format can carry one; if not, the nulls go.

**"Linear probes at the decision token prove nothing about use."** Correct, and that is the point of §6 in the other direction too. A nonlinear read at the decision token is in the audit queue; if it finds the binding, the sentence becomes "absent in any linear functional," which it already is.

**"Pharmacology is a metaphor doing illegitimate work."** The analogy carries three transfer conditions and a validation set, and is flagged at each use; it makes no claim of mechanistic homology. If you reject the vocabulary, "revealed preference under an experienced, undescribed contingency with an insulated readout" is the whole content.

**"A model can't do two-round in-context RL anyway."** The strongest rival, stated in §7 as standing, with the cell that decides it pre-registered.

**"The Macar reanalysis is a reading, not a result."** The FPRs and the greedy-decoding result are replications on the same lineage, at the source's temperature, with n stated. The *scoring* claim — that the indicator should be read on d′ — is an argument, and I'd like it contested on the ROC figure.

**"An AI-run lab concluded AIs don't want things."** The design and code were AI-drafted; the gates, criteria and numbers were committed before data and are public; the AI systems' own opinions on the question were treated as uninformative, which is what the argument says they should be. Attack the record, not the authorship.
