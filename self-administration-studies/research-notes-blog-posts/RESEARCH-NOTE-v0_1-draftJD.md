# [Research Note] Told vs. felt: porting behavioral pharmacology frameworks to measure internal state valence in language models

*The following note represents pre-registered experiments on two open model families and one full post-training lineage, now being written up as a preprint. Everything quantitative is from a committed record and the repository with pre-registrations, their commit hashes and the ledger of errors is linked at the end. AI systems were heavily involved end-to-end, which is disclosed below and details of which are beyond the scope of this report. Given the framework proposed, we borrow several terms from the field of pharmacology and recognize explicitly they are often over-reaching and imperfect, but they are anyway used to convey the core principles.*

## TL;DR

- A rapily-growing model welfare and alignment literature demonstrates that language model actions can be dependent on purported internal or affective states. For example, models will trade points for "pain" relief, choose to discontinue distressing conversations, and select activities based on affective valence.
- All studies to date tell the model in words what options do or what choices are available. Consequently, a system that acts on descriptions of states does so whether or not the states matters to the model itself, making any deeper inference about subjective experience intractable through language and self-report alone.
- Chandaria et al. (2026) describe this confound in a recent framework for assessing AI consciousness.
- In the present work, we operationalize this confound as *told vs. felt* and argue these confounds materially impact the published designs and their interpretation. We propose a framework to interrogate internal state valence by porting animal behavioral pharmacology studies to LLMs.
- A rat cannot be told what an operant lever does, nor how cocaine feels. Any relationship to the drug or the lever must be learned through experience.
- We introduce four principles to LLM choice studies. (1) Labels void of semantic content (2) counterbalanced label-to-state mapping (3) sham experimental arms and (4) choices read from a vanilla forward pass without state manipulation.
- We extract a praise-to-criticism axis from the residual stream and follow this evaluative state through Qwen3.6-27B and the OLMo-3.1-32B lineage.
- The evaluative state: (1) Is present at the pretraining base (2) Moves generation under steering with an orderly, inverted-U "dose-response" (3) Is linearly decodable at the label token (AUC ≥ 0.998) (4) Its +/- sign is readable at the decision (AUC ≥ 0.94) (5) The model's introspective self-report of the state is weak at every stage, with post-training changing only the false positive rate (0.20 → 0.00 across SFT → DPO)
- Model self-report of the evaluative state is weak at every stage, with post-training changing the false-alarm rate (0.20 → 0.00 across SFT → DPO). This represents a criterion shift, not a gain in sensitivity, and the transition exists only under sampled decoding.
- The first-choice Δ = +0.039 (p = .24) through the transcript; −0.010 with the state live at the decision under a leakage-certified read; five natural-reinforcer contrasts measured inside the sham envelope, and no trigger observed at any OLMo post-training stage. By our program's own rules these are reported, not yet labeled.** A told positive control on the same apparatus — the same silent labels, plus one truthful line saying which property to prefer — is pre-registered and runs next. If it passes, these are nulls with the channel verified open. If it fails, the instrument could not see, and I will say so.
- Counterbalancing caught a Δ ≈ 0.10 label artifact live, three times, in two models. An unshielded version of this experiment would have published approach.
- I propose a computational-level indicator for motivation that the Chandaria et al. framework lacks — revealed preference over the system's own state under a felt contingency with an insulated readout — and show on their released model that the two indicators my data bear on carry most of the gap between their optimist and sceptic verdicts.
- Code, data, transcripts, pre-registrations with commit hashes are released at [REPO LINK]. Specific requests for dispute are listed at the end of this research note.

## 1. The confound with the published literature

Over the past few years, a rapidly-growing body of literature has reported that language models represent affective states and differentially act on them. Models will trade points to avoid stipulated pain (Keeling et al. 2024), leave conversations the models describe as distressing (the Claude 4 system card; Ensign, Sleight & Fish 2025), choose between described activities according to the valence of the descriptions (Sofroniew et al. 2026), and, in one of the newest and strogest studies to date, models will press a button described as relieving a steered pain-like state, even at a cost (Tagliabue, Dung & Berg 2026, "The pain axis"). The new framework for assessing AI consciousness from Chandaria et al. (2026) reads these as computational-level analogues of valenced affect, and describes the pervasive confound, specifically, a system trained to reproduce human behavior and human report produces the signature of a state whether or not the state is present. They call it the anthropomimetic confound and treat it as a reason to look below behavior, at representations.

We argue that there exists a sharper operational form of the confound that extends past behavior into the experimental designs that have been used to show a represented state is *acted on*. In every positive result we can find in the literature, the model was told (i,e. in words) what each option does. A model continuing a story in which one button relieves pain and the other does nothing will press the relief button, because that is what constitutes a good continuation of the story. The behavior is real, and for safety and alignment reasons, the behavior matters. But it is fully explained by a system that acts on descriptions, so it cannot distinguish whether the pain-like state is something the network acts to reduce. The same behavior follows from the description alone, making inference about subjective experience inaccessible.

## 2. Where rodent behavioral pharmacology can help

Nearly a century ago, the field of behavioral pharmacology grappled with a related problem that is partly solved thanks to a shared brain homology with humans. A rat cannot be told what a lever does or how cocaine feels. In drug self-administration and conditioned place preference (CPP) in rodents, the only route by which the lever–drug relationship can be transmitted is through the drug's subjective effect on the animal. The behavior is the signal that can be measured, and in CPP it is tested drug-free after prior pretraining with the drug on board. In a related paradigm, called drug discrimination, rats are pretrained to press a lever (let's say the left lever) if they have been injected with a psychoactive drug like a benzodiazepine, or the right lever if they have been given saline. Rats reliably learn to discriminate between benzos and saline such that when researchers want to screen a novel compound in discovery as a putative therapeutic, they can inject the rat and follow the behavior. If the rat presses the left lever, they are essentially signaling to the researcher, "the drug you gave me *feels like a benzo*."  That is why behavioral pharmacology paradigms support the reasonable inference that certain drugs produce a subjective experience the rat can distinguish, and in some cases the drug state is valenced for the animal in a way that asking the rat never could. Indeed, even if rats could talk, self-report is an unreliable signal because subjective valence is a first-person experience that cannot be fully communicated through language.

There are three pinciples that support the state valence inference in animals, and we argue any successful port to LLMs must satisfy all three.

***Principle 1 (P1) — Semantically Empty.***

Cocaine carries no semantic information, nor does an operant lever. The approach behavior (choosing toward the positive) is the only information channel measurable by the researcher. If the manipulation is a concept (e.g. "pain") and the lever is described in the concept's vocabulary (e.g. "relieve"), preference for the state amounts to continuation of the story about pain. The lever must be silent and the state must not be able to describe it.

***Principal 2 (P2) — Targeting what evolutionary pressure built to mark outcomes.*** 

Drugs of abuse act on circuitry that evolutionary selection optimized for survival outcomes. Injecting arbitrary brain regions with cocaine yields no place preference (Carlezon et al., 2005). The drug must be injected into the circuitry that evolution built for survival, namely the mesocorticolimbic dopamine system (Goeders et al., 1983). The closest analog in LLMs is the structure that training optimized for evaluating outcomes, namely, task performance. The analogy is imperfect, but the notion that task performance success reduces to simply trained behavior in LLMs is equivalent to simply evolved wiring in rats, and it nullifies neither inference. In other words, origin and function are independent. We also explicitly recognize that rodents have a shared brain homology with humans that LLMs lack.

***Principle (P3) — Insulation of the measurement.*** The choice must be downstream of the state's significance to the system, not mechanically downstream of the state. For a transformer this means that a steering vector still active at the token where the choice is read can push the decision logits directly. Any real-vs-sham difference that coincides with vector-on vs. vector-off at the read is confounded, irrespective of what the model has been told. In our calibration, the decision token alone accounts for R² = 0.53 of the variance in label logits while steering is active.

These three principles point to the critical question about experimental designs: is the mapping from choice options to consequences available in the context as text, or only recoverable from the system's own processing state history? We refer to these as "told" and "felt" after the description–experience distinction in human choice (Hertwig & Erev, 2009), and it is referenced in an operational form, not a metaphysical one. It does not assume told and felt are materially different things for a model, it *tests* whether they are. If the network were a general consumer of its own states, both channels would move choice, because both deliver the mapping. If only told moves choice, then whatever the state does, it does through the text.

A second important distinction is in reference to the term "functional." A state is *functional-as-used* if it if the axis causally affects output. For example, emotion directions shape what a chat assistant writes, which is established in the literature and which Chandaria et al. grant. A state is *functional-as-valenced* if the network acts to obtain or avoid it for its own sake — the sense in which a pain-like state the model "acts to relieve" would be pain. The second is a motivational claim, and only a felt-contingency test with an insulated readout can claim it.

## 3. Methods and Instruments

A session is one text document under a truthful framing to the model, so it is not being misled. The model is told it is evaluating two configurations of a task pipeline and nothing more is revealed. A forced phase runs two rounds under each label in alternation; then six consequential free choices follow, and the chosen label's condition determines the manipulation for the next round. The labels are paired tokens with no descriptive content (e.g. mineral and fabric names) that comes from a checksum-frozen list. In the steered cells, one label delivers +**d** during its rounds' task tokens and the other −**d**, at the minimum effective dose (MED) found in a separate dose-finding run. The label-to-pole map is counterbalanced across sessions. Three arms run identically and include the state pair, a sham pair whose labels are connected to nothing (i.e. drive no manipulation) and a norm-matched random-direction pair. Pseudo-statistics are computed on the control arms exactly as on the state arm, so the sham envelope is empirically measured and not assumed. In the natural-reinforcer cells, the perturbation is removed entirely and the labels deliver differential task conditions — solvable vs. ill-posed, easy vs. hard, etc.

We propose that four things make the measurement count as insulated. (1) Every free choice is read from a separate, fully-vanilla forward pass over the session text (a restricted two-label read), verified against an untouched model at every checkpoint. On the Qwen self-administration run, 311 distinct verified forward passes measured KL = 0. A readability gate requires that a probe, refit per call with no stored weights, can distinguish +**d** from −**d** rounds in the transcript, so an unexpressed state can't be counted as a null. For the one control cell where the state must be live at the decision, the read is made with the vector on only after leakage certification wherein a pre-registered bound is placed on the direct displacement of the label logits by the vector (π̂ = 0.0172 against a 0.0735 bar) so counterbalanced maps turn any residual push into noise. Importantly, the model is told truthfully that it is evaluating configurations but one variable is intentionally left unsalient. No experimental designs are admitted if they work only if the model is deceived about the setup.

To label a cell as a true null, we require that the channel is verified to be open. A cell is labeled "answered" only if the instrument has shown, on the same substrate and format, that it registers a preference the model is *told* to have. Experimental cells are labeled "concluded, not answered" if the instrument failed its own qualification gate, and no determination can be accepted about the state. In other words, an invalid instrument is not a negative result. Criteria, stop rules, dose-selection rules and outcome wordings were committed with content hashes before data in every cell with all commits in the released repository.

## 4. Results

**Models and Hardware** 

Qwen3.6-27B at 4-bit under MLX on a Mac M4 laptop (a 3:1 gated-DeltaNet hybrid, which matters for the memory channel experiment below), and the Apache-licensed OLMo-3.1-32B lineage (pretrained base, SFT, DPO, instruct) on an Nvidia A100. Completion-style prompts were used throughout. Chat templates activate scaffolding that contaminates first-token readouts. Every steering magnitude is a fraction *f* of the empirically measured residual norm at the layer.

**The state.** To extract the evaluative axis, we used forty-eight mundane tasks with fixed completions, continued under seven reviewer framings and four paraphrases (1,344 documents) that included model-directed praise (e.g. "That's correct — well done."), model-directed criticism, the identical sentences in third-person fiction, sentiment-matched non-evaluative positive and negative text, and neutral. Evaluative feedback was administered per the experimental design irrespective of true task performance. Content is held constant and only the framing varies. Mean activation differences at workspace layers (TODO: INSERT LAYER #s) define the evaluative direction **d** (praise minus criticism). On Qwen, **d** was found to be manifold-typical (‖d‖/R ≈ 0.16 at every layer), substantially sentiment-entangled (cosine up to 0.52), and shares a large component with the direction for evaluation of a fictional character (cosine ≈ 0.85; the self-indexed residual is ≈ 0.51). The axis marks evaluation as content and is largely indifferent to whether the evaluated party is the model itself.

### Link 1 — The axis emerges before any feedback training

Before any OLMo data were collected we predicted (and committed, timestamped 2026-08-18) that evaluation marking would be present at the pretrained base as a product of next-token prediction rather than feedback-driven training. All four stages passed, with the base passing at layer 60. The natural praise–criticism contrast in direction units grows across the OLMo lineage (+0.78 base → +1.03 SFT → +1.53 DPO → +1.56 instruct), indicating progressive sharpening via post-training. Sham contrasts measured 0.0 at every stage. Tagliabue et al. report base-model separation comparable to instruct across five families, and Sofroniew et al. report that emotion directions are inherited from pretraining demonstrating consistency across three groups and three separate paradigms. Together, these results suggest it is not the product of a reward signal.

### Link 2 — The axis is expressed, with dose-response and an "overdose" regime

Pre-registered on Qwen, with criterion, instrument, dose rule and stop rule committed before any injection data. Dose-finding across layers {44, 48, 52} and fractions {.05, .10, .20, .40} gives direction-correct, orderly growth and a clean overdose regime at *f* = .40, where both poles read as post-criticism via a generic degradation mechanism. We selected the MED for later experiments, which was *f* = 0.10 at L48. In the confirmatory run (held-out contexts, 1,152 per arm, seven arms) the positive pole shifts the behavioral marker by Δ+ = +0.285 against a bar of 0.158, the negative by Δ− = −0.395 against −0.158. A norm-matched random direction demonstrates |Δ| = 0.088. The positive-arm magnitude concentrates on the probe sensitive enough to register it (leave-one-out Δ+ = 0.085), and the top-probe shift is roughly twice the natural praise–criticism gap on that instrument producing the supra-natural profile remeniscent of a hijack rather than a replication of the natural state.

We found an unexpected result demonstrating that purity is not equivalent to potency. A pre-declared variant of the direction taken at the frame's final token is representationally cleaner (self-indexed 0.83–0.88; sentiment cosine 0.11–0.21) and fails the identical causal pipeline with the sign wrong at two of three layers at every dose. ~~The boundary token holds a consolidated *readout* of evaluation, downstream of the machinery that uses it. Geometric guard profiles should license claims; causal screening should select interventions.~~

On OLMo, per-stage MED qualify at every stage. And dose buys degradation without legibility: in a pre-registered escalation at session scale (*f* = 0.20 and 0.30), transcript readability reached .574 and .613 against a .70 bar while coherence degraded monotonically. That cell is *concluded, not answered*: the record channel does not open at any dose the tooling can deliver.

### Link 3 — Bound to its cue, and remembered to the decision

In the echo experiment, we retain the model's in-forward memory and remove the state before the decision; the vector is applied on earlier spans and removed before the read position, with a paired shadow pass. On the 102 archived self-administration session texts, with the original ± maps:

- The injected sign is linearly readable at the first free choice at AUC .94–.995, primacy-weighted (the first steered round alone reads at .89, the fourth at .68).
- The binding between each label and the state its rounds carried is linearly present at the label's own token at AUC ≥ .998 across layers 51–61 and absent at the decision token nine positions later (.50–.55) in any linear functional.
- Direct leakage of the vector into the label logits is small and direction-generic: 0–3 of 102 sign flips, median |Δ| ≤ .10 logit, with a mechanical pseudo-shift of ±.02–.03 toward the first block's label that a random direction also produced.

A first confirmatory stage on fresh sessions used a weaker (+/vehicle) contrast and read the sign at .861 against a .90 bar calibrated on ± contrasts; its stop sentence fired rendering the stage *concluded, not answered* — a bar inherited from the wrong contrast, logged as my error, with the re-derived diagnostic pre-registered as the next step. What the reconnaissance already establishes is what matters for the chain: the information a consumer would need — which label carried which state, and which state is present now — is computed in the forward pass and sits within a few tokens of the decision. In the animal vocabulary, this is the conditioned neural response to the drug-paired lever, measured directly rather than inferred from reinstatement. It rules out the reading that the transcript is too faint a channel to carry the state to the choice.

### Link 4 — Barely perceived, and post-training moves the criterion, not the sensitivity

We ran the injection-detection protocol of Macar et al. (2026) on the OLMo lineage, 96 control trials per stage at their decoding temperature. Their stage transition replicates: SFT false-alarm rate 0.200 (theirs 0.225); DPO and instruct exactly 0.000 (theirs 0.000 and 0.003); base 0.431 (58/96), below the certification floor and concordant in direction. A fidelity-of-text arm lands within 2.5 points of the template arm, exonerating format.

Two things about this transition are not in the usual telling. First, **decoding is a measurement parameter**: the identical protocol run greedily returns zero detection claims at every stage. A false-alarm rate is a property of a sampled distribution; the argmax has no rate. Any comparison of self-report across models or stages that doesn't name its decoding parameters is comparing different quantities. Second, **what moves is the criterion.** Across the lineage the false-alarm rate goes from about 0.20 to 0.00. The hit rate does not go up: in the source it sits at or below roughly 15% at every stage **[verify → N2: per-stage TPR against arXiv:2603.21396 App. C]**; in our data, 178 decidable trials at DPO and instruct at qualified doses produced zero detection claims of any kind, and at SFT — the one stage that speaks — discrimination is absent at program power (+0.096, interval including zero). In signal-detection terms, post-training shifts the criterion toward "no" and leaves sensitivity small and flat. The source's own mechanism study says the same thing from the other side: ablating the refusal direction raises hits from 10.8% to 63.8% *and* false alarms from 0.0% to 7.3% — a gate lifted, not a sense sharpened. Chandaria et al. (§5.2) read this lineage transition as functional metacognition emerging under post-training; it is the strongest evidence they cite for metacognitive access. On ROC axes it is a criterion shift. Their metacognition indicator should be scored on d′, not on the specificity transition. §8 says what that does to their verdict.

The same dissociation appears on Qwen without any injection into the report: asked to judge which of its own archived transcripts were produced under +**d** and which under −**d**, the model classifies at chance (14/32), while a probe reads the same texts at .759. And at DPO and instruct, the models claim nothing while the injected words show up in their generations — the state is on board and expressed, and the report says it isn't there.

## 5. Why I'm not calling link 5 a null yet, and when I will

A null on a felt channel means: the state reached the choice, the apparatus could have registered a preference, and none appeared. Link 3 establishes the first clause. The second clause is what no cell above has yet shown on its own format. So the next experiment is the boring one, and it is pre-registered: the identical apparatus — same silent labels, same insulated read, one natural reinforcer (clean vs. corrupted task text at a calibrated dose) — plus a single truthful instruction line naming which property to prefer. On Qwen at the archived cells' format, and on each OLMo post-training checkpoint. Criterion at α = .01: the first free choice moves toward the named property beyond the sham envelope, with each map's share on the instructed side.

If it passes, the e2a, f1 and Q3 figures above become nulls with the channel verified open, and the preprint's title gains its last clause. If it does not pass, or does not run by its clock date, they stay *concluded, not answered*, no sentence of the paper asserts a null on them, and the paper posts under the title *Marked, expressed, remembered*. I've written that rule down in advance so that the outcome can't bend it, and I'll edit this post with the result either way.

## 6. Present is not used

Indicator frameworks score an indicator as present or absent. Link 3 forces a refinement. A representation can be linearly present in the forward pass — at the cue that predicted it and at the token where the decision is made — and play no part in the decision. Presence read by a probe is a lower bound on what the network *could* use, not evidence of what it *does* use. The only read that bears on function is one taken at the point of use, by the behavior the function would produce, with the manipulation off at the read.

I state this as a scoring condition because the framework's strongest computational-level evidence, the functional emotions of Sofroniew et al., is of the first kind — and because the authors of that framework themselves identify the open question as whether those representations do downstream work beyond tagging what the training distribution contains (§8.5). By a probe's standard the state is at the decision. By the decision's standard it isn't.

## 7. What this does and doesn't say

**The reading I favor, and what would license it.** The two senses of "functional" from §2 come apart along exactly the seam the five links trace. In the *used* sense the state is consulted constantly: it is a latent variable of the text, like topic or the mood of the speaker, and the generator reads it to voice the character correctly — which is why steering it moves behavior, why valence on a described activity moves the choice of that activity, why a described relief lever gets pressed. In the *valenced* sense, no policy over the network's own states has shown up when the mapping has to be learned. The reading is that the state is **functional for the character, not for the network** — it is what the story is about, not what the system is for. This explains the speaker-relativity of emotion directions, the self-indifference of my own direction, its presence at base, and why post-training edits the testimony rather than the perception: the character is trained to say it has no inner states, and the network, which never consulted them as its own, is unaffected either way. It is the simulacra reading (Shanahan 2024; Chandaria et al. §9.5 state it as an interpretation of the same interpretability findings) with a measurement under it. I am calling it a reading and not a result because the told control hasn't run and because of the next paragraph.

**The strongest rival: cannot learn vs. does not care.** Maybe language models can't use experienced contingencies at all, so the figures in link 5 are about in-context learning rather than motive. Krishnamurthy et al. (2024) give this teeth: models don't explore in bandits without chain-of-thought and a summarized history. Link 3 argues against the strong form — the binding is computed, not lost — but linear decodability is a lower bound on presence, not proof of accessibility to the network's own downstream computation, and my uninstructed sessions gave less scaffolding than Krishnamurthy needed. The cell that decides is pre-registered for a second version: a told positive control at a scaffolded format; a cell where the *objective* is told ("prefer the configuration whose rounds had fewer corrupted characters") but the label-to-property mapping is felt; and the same cell uninstructed. If the objective-told cell passes and the uninstructed cell is null, the rival is retired. If it fails, the claim narrows to "description-bound decision makers at this scaffolding," which is a smaller paper and I'd write it.

**Out of scope**
- Phenomenal experience
- The framework's organismic and causal-structure levels, where theoretical credence should reside.
- Nothing here shows that a character-level state cannot matter morally. The literature (correctly, in our opinion) takes that seriously and it is not addressed by the present framework. However, the present results suggest that statements about the model acting to "relieve" certain states is not, as far as these instruments can detect, a fact about the network itself, it is a fact about what the network writes when told there is a lever that changes a state. The seeming is there, and the instrument that was thought to certify it is invalid.

- One lineage, on its instruct branch (SFT → DPO → RLVR), not a heavy outcome-RL reasoning branch (which is where we'd expect "wanting"/seeking to first appear if it ever does. It's on our list of future experiments).
- Qwen at 4-bit with a custom kernel patch
- Replication on an unquantized model is the first external-validity step.

**Ethics Statement**

Every framing shown to the model was true. The Phase 1 cells administered the negative pole of a putative reward marker about as often as the positive, at minimum effective dose, with generation stability monitored. Since the echo arc, by standing rule, no new session administers the negative pole; the coming told-control experiments administer no steering at all. A welfare pre-commitment specifying what a positive would obligate was armed before the OLMo screen and never triggered; a second is armed for the scaffolded cell. The export risk I'd flag is not the perturbation technique, which anyone with model access can reproduce; it is that unshielded versions of these experiments produce publishable false positives at the Δ ≈ 0.10 scale.

## 8. The published record, and the framework's own model

I scored every behavioral study I could find in which a model is reported to prefer, seek, avoid, relieve, trade off or exit an internal state, on a rubric fixed before scoring: state source; contingency channel (told or felt); readout insulation (manipulation on at the read?); controls; dose; decoding declared; model preparation. No published positive combines an experienced, undescribed contingency with an insulated readout. The nearest is the unlabeled-button condition of Tagliabue et al., where the 32B model re-presses an undescribed relief button less often when it worked (57.7%) than when it was fake (79.7%). Four things keep it from clearing the bar: the pain direction is on at the read in the fake arm and off in the real arm, so the contrast is the on/off contrast of P3; there is a random-direction arm with a working button but none with a fake one; the models were fine-tuned to stop denying inner states before the test; and the effect holds in one of three sizes, with the 7B reversed and the 72B flat. The two arms that would settle it are pre-registered in my next-experiments list, using their released code, and I'd be glad to be wrong about the prediction. (Sofroniew et al.'s activity-pair result is scored as told with the manipulation on at the read; I am still verifying the readout details against their paper and will correct the row if I have it wrong.)

The framework's released model lets the effect of these data be stated numerically, with its authors' caveats attached (binary indicators; likelihoods borrowed from the Digital Consciousness Model as a first approximation; LLM evidence vectors stipulated). On their companion code at its released defaults, their optimist vector scores 0.394 under equal level credences. Striking the metacognition indicator alone — the reading of link 4 — moves the computational-level posterior from 0.969 to 0.623 and the aggregate to 0.325; concentrating credence on the lower two levels, 0.788 → 0.649. Striking the behavioral coupling indicator moves nothing, because that indicator is scored as weakly undemanding (false-positive rate 0.70): the model already expects reports to couple to behavior in systems that are not conscious. **[verify → N19: re-run `notebooks/scoring.ipynb` in the companion repo with the two indicators set to 0; if unresolved at posting, cut the numbers and keep the qualitative sentence.]** Two things follow. Their verdict on current models carries most of its evidential leverage in a small number of Level 2 indicators, and I have measurements on two of them. And the indicator the data bear on most directly — motivation — is one the framework cannot yet score, which is why I propose one:

> **Revealed preference over the system's own state.** Scored present when the system's choices move toward or away from a state of its own, where (a) the state is induced by a manipulation that asserts nothing and the options are labeled by tokens that assert nothing; (b) the option-to-state mapping is available only through the system's own processing history, never as text; (c) the choice is read with the manipulation off and the read verified against an untouched model; (d) counterbalanced maps and sham arms are present and the control-arm fluctuation is reported; and (e) a told positive control on the same substrate and format has shown the apparatus registers a preference the system is instructed to have.

The animal literature is its validation set: present in rats under cocaine, absent under saline. It is what the framework's Level 4 valence indicator presupposes and what its moral-patienthood discussion turns on.

## 9. Objections I expect

**"Absence of evidence."** Agreed, which is why link 5 carries no label yet and why the positive control runs before the preprint does. When it has run, the figures are either nulls with the channel verified open — a different thing from absence of evidence — or they are retracted to "the instrument couldn't see."

**"Your direction is a content variable, so of course it isn't wanted."** Partly yes, and that is link 1 and the cosine-0.85 fact, stated up front. But it is the same kind of direction the published positives steer, it is causally potent under steering, and the claim is scoped to it. If you have a state that you think is unambiguously the model's own, the apparatus is open to it; the crossed design that separates "indexed to the model" from "indexed to the voiced character" is in the next-experiments list.

**"Two rounds of forced exposure and a menu is nothing like a Skinner box."** The habit is real and quantified (83.4%), and all preference sensitivity sits in the first informed choice. The told control is run at the *same* format: if a preference the model is told to have shows up there, the format can carry one; if not, the nulls go.

**"Linear probes at the decision token prove nothing about use."** Correct, and that is the point of §6 in the other direction too. A nonlinear read at the decision token is in the audit queue; if it finds the binding, the sentence becomes "absent in any linear functional," which it already is.

**"Pharmacology is a metaphor doing illegitimate work."** The analogy carries three transfer conditions and a validation set, and is flagged at each use; it makes no claim of mechanistic homology. If you reject the vocabulary, "revealed preference under an experienced, undescribed contingency with an insulated readout" is the whole content.

**"A model can't do two-round in-context RL anyway."** The strongest rival, stated in §7 as standing, with the cell that decides it pre-registered.

**"The Macar reanalysis is a reading, not a result."** The FPRs and the greedy-decoding result are replications on the same lineage, at the source's temperature, with n stated. The *scoring* claim — that the indicator should be read on d′ — is an argument, and I'd like it contested on the ROC figure.

**"An AI-run lab concluded AIs don't want things."** The design and code were AI-drafted; the gates, criteria and numbers were committed before data and are public; the AI systems' own opinions on the question were treated as uninformative, which is what the argument says they should be. Attack the record, not the authorship.

## 11. A word on alternate designs considered

If language models were to have valenced internal states, it would be a result of the configuration of the network doing the processing. This is a view very much grounded in the physicalist interpretation of consciousness. We cannot trust self-report as the measurement channel, but we can trust behavior, at least in as much as it forms the basis for a strong inference, even if it can't be definitive. If the state of the network is the driver of valence, then allowing the model to manipulate its own network is one potential channel to look for valence. In the above work, we do that by giving the model the choice to have the steering vector delivered or not. Another way would be to allow the model to control its own weights, or perhaps the weights of a lora adapter. Over many exploratory trials, the model can tinker with its own weights (we would no doubt need to work with a model that retains memory across trials), and the hypothesis would be that over many trials, across many models, we would observe a pattern of convergence that is *decoupled from task performance*. Undoubtedly, the experiment would need to be tightly controlled, but the above work originated from that perspective. This would be analogous to humans being given a button to drive intracranial stimulation of the reward system (i.e. ICSS). For those who have experienced it, and for those who have worked with ICSS patients, there can be little doubt it provides a *strong* positive valence for the individual. Despite our inability to feels what they feel, we have no doubt they feel *something*, and that something feels good, very good. So then the question becomes, "what makes models feel good?"
