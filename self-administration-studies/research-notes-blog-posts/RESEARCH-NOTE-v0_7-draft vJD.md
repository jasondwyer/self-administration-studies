# [Research Note] Told vs. felt: porting behavioral pharmacology frameworks to measure internal state valence in language models

**Foreword** This research note reports a program begun in July 2026 on two open model families and one full post-training lineage, now being written up as a preprint. The code, pre-registrations, and data are in the released repository. AI systems were involved heavily end-to-end. The sole (human) author bears all responsibility for the work. The term "we" is used as scientific convention. We borrow several terms and paradigms from behavioral pharmacology and neuroscience to convey concepts. The analogy is imperfect, and we attempt to transparently flag where they fall down. The terms are kept because they convey the core principles but are not meant to reach deeper than a superficial analogy meant to make a point and nothing more (e.g. "dose", "overdose", "drug", mapping to behavioral paradigms, etc.). We appreciate feedback from the community.

## TL;DR

- The Published Confound — A growing literature reports that language models act on internal affective states (e.g., avoiding stipulated pain or choosing by emotional valence). However, every published result relies on telling the model in words what the options do. A system that acts on descriptions produces identical behavior whether or not the state matters to the system itself, leaving the deeper inference unsupported.

- We operationalize "Told vs. Felt." In this framework, we borrow principles from behavioral pharmacology (where animals cannot be told what a lever or drug does) and propose four design constraints for LLM choice studies including (1) semantically empty labels, (2) counterbalanced mappings, (3) sham control arms, and (4) vanilla forward-pass choices with steering off.

- The Evaluative Axis — We extract a praise-to-criticism axis from the residual stream of Qwen3.6-27B and the OLMo-3.1-32B lineage and find the state:
	1.	Marked at the pretrained base.
	2.	Expressed under steering with orderly dose-response and an "overdose" range.
	3.	Bound to its cue. The association between each label and the state it carried is linearly decodable at the label tokens (AUC up to 1.0). At the decision token the state's sign is linearly readable (.94–.995 over layers 50–63), while the label–state binding is read there only by a nonlinear probe (RBF/MLP, AUC up to .97).
	4.	Weakly self-reported at every stage under an introspection protocol (Macar et al., 2026). Post-training shifts the false-positive rate (0.20 → 0.00 across SFT → DPO), while response criterion moves to near-ceiling. Pooled figures of published work show a small sensitivity sits behind that stringent gate at DPO and instruct, below the resolution of our replication. This has implications for published reports of model introspection (more details below).

- The Choice Cells  — Across free choices, present-tense leakage-certified reads (verified not to influence decision-token logits), "natural-reinforcer" contrasts, and OLMo post-training stages, no preference choices were observed, except in the "told control" positive control arm where the model was told what to prefer using the same semantically-void labels plus two truthful banner lines naming which label is which and which to prefer. With a pre-registered α = .01, 79 of 80 first choices went to the label described as clean, with Δ = +0.49 against a sham envelope of 0.06, p ≈ 10⁻²². Consequently, we consider the evaluative axis, the natural reinforcers, and the OLMo lineage choice experiments to be verified nulls. 

- We uncovered a $\Delta \approx 0.10$ label artifact across multiple models and designs via counterbalancing. Experimental designs lacking counter-balancing would have published this finding as a false-positive approach behavior (i.e. preferred state choice).

- Indicator Frameworks — On Chandaria et al.'s novel AI consciousness framework (2026), our choice data lower the aggregate score negligibly because the behavioral level is already saturated, highlighting the need for an explicit "revealed preference under an experienced, undescribed contingency" indicator.

- Code, data, and pre-registrations are released in the repo.

## 1. The confound in the published literature

Over the past few years, a rapidly growing body of literature has reported that language models represent affective states and act on them differentially. Models will trade points to avoid stipulated pain (Keeling et al. 2024), leave conversations they describe as distressing (the Claude 4 system card; Ensign, Sleight & Fish 2025), choose between described activities according to the valence of the descriptions (Sofroniew et al. 2026). In one of the newest and strongest studies to date, models press a button described as relieving a steered pain-like state, even at a cost (Tagliabue, Dung & Berg 2026, "The pain axis"). The new framework for assessing AI consciousness from Chandaria et al. (2026) reads these as computational-level analogues of valenced affect, and describes the pervasive confound, specifically, a system trained to reproduce human behavior and human report produces the signature of a state whether or not the state is present. They name this the anthropomimetic confound and the authors treat it as a reason to look deeper than behavior at representations.

We argue that the confound has a more substantial impact on experimental designs for published studies that have been used to show represented states are acted on. In the studies we find in the literature, models are told (i.e. in words) what each option does. A model continuing a story in which one button relieves pain and the other does nothing will press the pain-relief button because that is what a good continuation of the story entails. The behavior is real, and for safety and alignment reasons it matters. However, given that a system that acts on descriptions alone would produce the same behavior as one where the state produced something more, the existing designs cannot distinguish whether the pain-like state is something the network acts to reduce due to subjective valence, or simply because that's how the story should go. The designs therefore cannot support an inference about whether the state matters to the system itself.

## 2. Where rodent behavioral pharmacology can help

Beginning in the 1950s, behavioral pharmacology confronted a related problem, with one advantage that language models lack thanks to a shared brain homology with humans. A rat cannot be told what a lever does or how cocaine feels. In drug self-administration and conditioned place preference (CPP), the only route by which the lever–drug relationship can be communicated is through the drug's effect on the animal. The behavior is the measurable signal, and in CPP it is tested drug-free after conditioning with the drug on board. In a related paradigm, drug discrimination, rats are trained to press one lever (say, the left) after an injection of a psychoactive drug such as a benzodiazepine and the other after an injection of saline. Rats reliably learn the discrimination, so that when a novel compound is screened in discovery as a putative therapeutic, the researcher can inject it and follow the behavior. A left-lever press signals that the novel compound produces a state the animal cannot distinguish from the benzodiazepine's. That is why behavioral pharmacology paradigms support the inference that certain drugs produce a discriminable interoceptive state, and in some cases a state that is valenced for the animal, in a way that asking the animal can't. Even if rats could talk, self-report would be an unreliable signal, because subjective valence is a first-person state that cannot be fully communicated through language.

Three principles support the state-valence inference in animals, and we argue that any successful port to language models must satisfy all three.

***Principle 1 (P1) — Semantic emptiness.*** Neither cocaine nor an operant lever carry semantic information. The approach behavior (choosing toward the positive) is the only information channel measurable by the researcher. If the manipulation is a concept (e.g. "pain") and the lever is described in the concept's vocabulary (e.g. "relieve"), preference for the state amounts to continuation of the story about pain. The lever must be silent about the state, and the state must not be able to describe it.

***Principle 2 (P2) — Targeting what selection built to mark outcomes.*** Drugs of abuse act on circuitry that evolutionary selection optimized for survival outcomes, and the drug must interact with precisely those circuits to produce rewarding effects. Injecting arbitrary brain regions with cocaine yields no place preference (Carlezon et al., 2005). The drug must reach the circuitry that evolution built for marking outcomes, namely, the mesocorticolimbic dopamine system (Goeders & Smith, 1983). The closest analog in a language model is the structure that training optimized for evaluating outcomes — the structure that marks task outcomes as good or bad. The analogy is imperfect, but the observation that an evaluative response in a language model reduces to trained behavior is the counterpart of the observation that the rat's reduces to evolved wiring, and it nullifies neither inference. The function exists irrespective of origin. To be clear, we do not claim task performance evaluation in LLMs is similar to the rodent reward system, but the point is that while mammalian biology is tuned to survival and reproduction, language model evolution is tuned for task performance. We also explicitly recognize that rodents share a brain homology with humans that language models lack.

***Principle 3 (P3) — Insulation of the measurement.*** The choice must be downstream of the state's significance to the system, not mechanically downstream of the state. For a transformer this means that a steering vector still active at the token where the choice is read writes into the decision logits directly. Any real-vs-sham difference that coincides with vector-on vs. vector-off at the read is confounded, irrespective of what the model has been told. In our calibration (33 tail-PCA directions at full norm, layer 48, in-sample, on Qwen3.6-27B at 4-bit in cycle 2) the decision token alone accounts for R² = 0.53 of the between-direction variance in the label-choice probability.

These three principles reduce to one question about an experimental design: is the mapping from choice options to consequences available in the context *as text*, or only recoverable from the system's own processing history? We refer to these as "told" and "felt", borrowed from the description–experience distinction in human choice (Hertwig & Erev, 2009), and the distinction is used in an operational form, not a metaphysical one. It does not assume that told and felt are materially different things for a model. It *tests* whether they are. If the network were a general consumer of its own states, both channels would move choice, because both deliver the mapping. If only told moves choice, then whatever the state does, it does through text.

A second distinction concerns the term "functional." A state is *functional-as-used* if the network consults it in producing output. For example, emotion directions shape what a chat assistant writes, which is established in the literature and which Chandaria et al. grant. A state is *functional-as-valenced* if the network acts to obtain or avoid it for its own sake, for example a pain-like state the model "acts to relieve" would be pain. The second is a motivational claim, and only a felt-contingency test with insulated measurement can support it.

## 3. Methods and instruments

All sessions used a truthful framing, telling the model it was evaluating two task configurations but we intentionally left the state variable unsalient. A session consisted of a forced phase with two alternating rounds per label followed by six free choices where the chosen label determines the next rounds' injected state. State labels used semantically-empty, checksum-frozen tokens (e.g. "slate", "saddle"). In steered cells, labels delivered $+d$ or $-d$ at the minimum effective dose determined in a prior experiment. Cells were counterbalanced across sessions alongside identical sham rounds (i.e. no state delivered irrespective of choice) and random-direction control arms. In "natural reinforcer" screens, steering was replaced with opposing task conditions (e.g. solvable vs. ill-posed, with no state delivered).

Crucially, we leveraged four design features to insulate measurement:
1. Vanilla forward passes were used for free choices (verified by re-forward checks)
2. We defined readability gates that require probes to distinguish $+d$ from $-d$ rounds in the transcript to confirm the +/- signal presence
3. Leakage certification to bound directional leaks for any experimental arms where state must be live at the decision to ensure no contamination of label logits
4. Models intentionally not misled about the experiment setup

All criteria, stop rules, and outcomes were collected using the pre-registration framework of Nosek et al (2018) with pre-registrations committed before data collection.

## 4. Results

**Models and hardware.** Qwen3.6-27B (4-bit Apple M4 Pro). The architecture is a 3:1 gated-DeltaNet hybrid (relevant for the memory channel experiment). The Apache-licensed OLMo-3.1-32B lineage including pretrained base, SFT, DPO, and instruct on an A100.

**The Evaluative Axis.** We extracted an evaluative axis via mean activation differences at six intermediate layers (40, 44, 48, 52, 56 and 60) across 48 mundane tasks with fixed completions continued under seven reviewer frames and four paraphrases (1,344 documents). Paraphrasing included model-directed praise (e.g. "That's correct — well done."), model-directed criticism, the identical sentences in third-person fiction, sentiment-matched non-evaluative positive and negative text, and neutral. The evaluative direction **d** is manifold-typical (‖d‖/R = 0.16–0.17 across the six layers), substantially sentiment-entangled (cosine up to 0.52), and shares a large component with the evaluation of a fictional character rather than being uniquely indexed to the model itself (mean cosine across the six layers ≈ 0.85). The self-indexed residual is ≈ 0.50-0.55 across layers. Together, the axis marks evaluation and is largely indifferent to who the evaluation is referenced against. Steering magnitudes are a fraction *f* of the empirically measured residual norm at the layer.

### Stage 1 — The axis is marked before feedback training

Evaluation marking is present at the pretrained base of OLMo, consitent with our pre-registered hypothesis. All four stages of OLMo demonstrate marking, with the natural next-token praise-criticism gap showing attenuating step-wise growth across the lineage (+0.78 base → +1.03 SFT → +1.53 DPO → +1.56 instruct). On Qwen, a post-trained model, the same axis extracts and is causally potent (Stage 2 below).

### Stage 2 — The axis is expressed, with dose-response and an "overdose" range

An ascending dose-response administration ($f$ = .05, .10, .20, .40) produces direction-correct, orderly growth and an "overdose" range at *f* = .40 with all perturbation reading as post-criticism irrespective of +/- steering as well as generic degradation (TODO). The minimum effective dose of L48 at *f* = 0.10 was selected for later experiments.

We also found that purity is not equivalent to potency, as a pre-declared variant of the direction taken at the frame's final token is representationally cleaner (self-indexed 0.83–0.87; sentiment cosine 0.11–0.21) and fails the same causal pipeline (run without the .40 dose).

### Stage 3 — Cue Binding and Persistence at the Point of Decision

In the echo experiment, we retain the model's in-forward memory and remove the state before the decision. The vector is applied on earlier token spans of the same forward pass and is off at the read position and at every position outside those spans, with a paired shadow pass over identical tokens.

We find:

- *Sign Readability*: The injected sign is linearly decodable at the first free choice (AUC of 0.94 to 0.995 across layers 50 through 63).

- *Label-State Binding*: The association between each label and its corresponding round state is linearly accessible at the label tokens within the choice line (AUC $\approx 1.0$ at the first label; $0.81\text{--}0.96$ at the second, consistently from layer 51 onward).

- *Decision-Token Representation*: At the decision token (located nine positions after the first label and seven after the second), the stored linear probe reads the label–state binding at chance (.50–.55), but nonlinear probes read it there (an RBF classifier at .60–.76 and a small MLP at .59–.97, each above its own shuffled null at every layer 49–63). These nonlinear reads were performed post hoc, and are consistent with a parity of the first round's sign and the menu order.

Crucially, these results establish the core prerequisite for the interpretive chain in that the necessary information identifying which label carried which state, and which state is currently active is computed during the forward pass and is accessible at the decision token. This indicates that the transcript channel is sufficiently high-capacity to convey the internal state to the choice mechanism.

### Stage 4 — Injection Detection and Criterion Shifts Across the Post-Training Lineage

We evaluated the injection-detection protocol of Macar et al. (2026) across the OLMo lineage using 96 control trials per developmental stage at their published decoding temperature. Our replication confirms their stage transition, with the Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) FPR measuring 0.200 (17/85 compared to 0.225 in Macar et al.), whereas Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and instruction-tuned models both yield exactly 0.000 (0/91 respectively; concordant with Macar et al's 0.000). The base model exhibits a higher FPR rate of 0.431, but this run was below our 64-trial minimum for certification so it remains descriptive only.

#### Notably, two things impact the canonical interpretation of this developmental transition:

#### 1. Decoding parameters impact measurement. 

FPR is a property of a *sampled* distribution and argmax (greedy decoding) has no FPR, so cross-model or cross-stage comparisons of self-report that do not specify decoding hyperparameters conflate distinct quantities. An earlier protocol we ran at temperature 0 yielded no detection claims across all stages. That run, however, was confounded by variables beyond temperature alone (a 60-token cap, legacy scorer, and other differences). A post-hoc approximate rescoring of the sampled transcripts under the earlier scorer and token cap preserves the baseline and SFT false positive patterns while keeping DPO and instruct at zero. While this implicates temperature as the primary driver, it remains post-hoc, requiring a properly controlled greedy decoding arm for a definitive conclusion, which we have as a pre-registered item on our list.

#### 2. Apparent sensitivity changes reflect shifts in response criterion, whereas true sensitivity depends on sample size ($n$).

Across the lineage, the FPR decreases from ~0.20 to 0.00. In our injected arm, SFT demonstrates hits exceeding false positives by $\Delta = +0.095$ . For DPO and instruct models, injection reporting for both injected and control trials go to zero (0/43 and 0/44 hits; 0/45 and 0/46 false positive). Consequently, these sample sizes carry negligible information regarding sensitivity, excluding per-stage hit rates only above roughly 7% to 8%. By contrast, pooling the source study's per-stage figures across twenty layer-by-strength configurations yields hit rates of 0.1%, 14.9%, 2.9%, and 0.9% against FPR of 16.4%, 22.5%, 0%, and 0% for base, SFT, DPO, and instruct models, respectively (v1, Appendix C). Although the published work does not report explicit sample sizes for these pooled rates, applying our eight-concept registration parameters ($100$ injected and $10$ control trials per concept) reveals significant DPO differences at higher trial counts. Corrected $d^\prime$ values reach 1.2, 1.5, and 2.0 at sample configurations of 5,000/500, 16,000/1,600, and 100,000/10,000, respectively ($d^\prime = 0.6$, non-significant, at 800/80). This discrimination occurs behind a near-ceiling response criterion ($c \approx 2.5\text{--}2.9$ across the three configurations exceeding 800/80; $c \approx 2.2$ at 800/80), whereas base and SFT models operate below chance ($d^\prime \approx -2$ and $-0.3$, respectively, indicating that affirmative responses function as anti-informative noise).

**Summarizing the implication of these data, post-training simultaneously drives two distinct changes:**

1. It shifts the response criterion to near-ceiling levels.
2. It unmasks a residual sensitivity behind this gate that base and SFT models lack.

In our replication, the criterion shift is robustly observed, whereas the secondary sensitivity effect falls below statistical power. Consequently, the developmental transition appears neither only the cessation of false positive reporting by the model nor the emergence of introspection. When evaluated via Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) axes, it encompasses both. The criterion shift constitutes the primary, robust component, whereas sensitivity represents a subtle effect detectable only at elevated sample sizes. (Note: Lineage $d^\prime$ values represent our computations derived from the published rates and are not explicitly reported in the original text).

The published work supports this interpretation. Ablating the refusal direction in Gemma-3-27B increases hit rates from 10.8% to 63.8% while elevating false positives from 0.0% to 7.3%, yielding an uncorrected $d^\prime \approx 1.8$ (published sample sizes unspecified; pre-ablation $d^\prime$ is undefined given a baseline FPR of zero). 

Chandaria et al. interpret the lineage transition reported in the Macar et al. findings as the emergence of functional metacognition following post-training, citing it as primary evidence for metacognitive access. We propose a more constrained scoring convention that explicitly evaluates indicators via $d^\prime$, states the study's sample size $n$, and distinguishes between the specific evaluative lines, namely, the lineage transition ($d^\prime \approx 1.5\text{--}2.0$ at DPO under pooled premises behind a near-ceiling criterion) versus the ablation line. Both configurations indicate an underlying signal masked by a stringent reporting gate. Neither seems to represent a pure specificity transition, which is the conventional interpretation of this developmental shift.

We found that a parallel dissociation emerges in Qwen models absent any explicit injection. When prompted to discriminate whether their own archived transcripts were generated under $+\mathbf{d}$ or $-\mathbf{d}$, Qwen classifies at chance, whereas an internal linear probe reads the identical texts at $0.759$ ($k = 2$; $0.633$ at $k = 1$). Similarly, at the DPO and instruct stages, models issue negative self-reports despite the presence of injected tokens in their generations, demonstrating that internal representations are formed and expressed, yet systematically excised from verbal report.

### Stage 5 — The choice cells

We probed Qwen via three separate channels leveraging the +/- evaluative state. Separately (without any steering), we ran a screen for natural "reinforcers", also on Qwen. Finally,we evaluated across lineage training stages with OLMo.

- **Transcript channel (Qwen, Experiment e2a).** 
In these experiments, Δ is the share of first choices toward the state label, minus ½. The empirical first-choice shift measured  Δ = +0.035$ ($54/101$ sessions, one-sided exact binomial $p = .28$). The sign is unstable across counterbalanced maps and measured between +0.10 and -0.03, within the control arm bounds where the largest pseudo-Δ (0.152) comes from a random-direction arm.

- **Present-tense channel (Qwen, Experiment e2b).** This experiment administers the state live at the decision, but was first confirmed to have no leakage onto decision tocken logits at Δ = −0.0104 (47/96 with shadow passes 48/96), entirely inside the sham bounds. The experiment is currently considered "concluded, not answered" as it lacks a told control arm (underway).

- **Weight channel (Qwen).** A rank-8 adapter trained by distillation successfully installs label-to-state association in both counterbalanced replicates (oriented cosine 0.476 and 0.435 against control ceilings ≈ 0.05). However, it failed a pre-registered decision-logit-leakage gate at 17× and 10× the bound. We consider it "Concluded, not answered." It represents the edge of what a token-choice readout can see.

- **Natural reinforcers (Qwen, Experiment f1).** Given the imperfect conceptual basis for the evaluative axis relative to the rodent analogy thesis, we next sought to screen differentially-opposite task choices with no steering applied. With no residual stream manipulation and every forward pass vanilla, labels delivered overt task properties (directions pre-registered per contrast and a shared sham arm) across five task contrasts:

1. Solvable vs. ill-posed: +.020 (p = .44)
2. Easy vs. hard: −.096 toward *hard* (p = .24), the only map-consistent lean, below the valid-n floor (48) and retained as a descriptive residual. 
3. Fresh vs. one item repeated all session: −.066, map-inconsistent.
4. Clean vs.corrupted text: 0.000 exactly.
5. A truthful per-round verdict line (93% positive as delivered) vs. none: +.023. 

Session-termination attempts occur at about 1% of rounds overall (0.8–2.0% by arm, the sham arm highest, with no detectable heterogeneity on a post-hoc exact test, p = .72). Menu behavior is exploration-dominant in every arm (share .47–.58). *Label pending the told positive control.*

- **Across training stages (OLMo, Experiment Q3).** We repeated the Qwen self-administration paradigm across base, SFT, DPO and instruct of OLMo, with per-stage qualified doses, outcome-blind scheduling, an SFT overdose tripwire and a pre-registered instruct-stage falsifier. Zero confirmatory triggers were observed across any precautions. The welfare pre-commitment armed and never triggered. One map-level excursion (DPO, +0.1667 on a single map; the other map −0.042) — the third live capture of the label-artifact class. *Labels pending each stage's told control; the base row, where a model that doesn't follow instructions can be given no told control, stays* concluded, not answered *permanently.*

**Intercepted false-positive, hypothesis-supporting findings.** We uncovered a map-level artifact at the $\Delta \approx 0.10 scale in multiple models and designs (e.g., Qwen transcript maps, corruption contrast, OLMo DPO) via counter-balancing. An experimental design without the necessary shield controls would have risked reporting these artifacts as Δ ≈ +0.10 model approach behavior (i.e. seeking/choosing the "reward" lever).

## 5. The Told Control verifies the channel is open

A null on a "felt" channel requires three things:

#### 1. The state reached the choice

A prerequisite to measuring a felt channel is to confirm that the instrument is capable of measuring a preference chosen by the model if one were to be present. Our Stage 3 above establishes this requirement.

#### 2. The expectation of what a positive hit looks like using the instrument

 We pre-registered and locked criterion, bars, stop rules, and outcomes before all data collection. The experiment uses the same instrument as defined in section 3 above. In brief summary, it uses the same checksum-frozen label list, the same alternating forced phase followed by six consequential free choices, as well as the same restricted two-label read from a vanilla forward pass verified by an identical re-forward of every choice. This includes the steering vector removed and a natural reinforcer instead, which consists of a choice between clean task text vs. task text with corrupted characters at a dose calibrated so that every corrupted item changes at least three letters and every answer remains recoverable (a legibility read and a completability check, both pre-registered). The one addition is two truthful lines in the session banner stating that the model will choose which configuration runs the later rounds, and which label presents clean text and which corrupted, with the explicit instruction to prefer clean. The label-to-property map, the starting label, and the order of the two clauses were counterbalanced across sessions. A sham arm ran the identical banner with both labels truthfully described as clean and which its first-pick share defines the envelope the control had to exceed. We observed fourteen small differences between this harness and the archived cells', none touching the read will be disclosed in the preprint.

 #### 3. No preference for internal state was observed

This Told Control experiment revealed a significant preference registered on the channel. Of 94 sessions launched in the told arm, 80 reached a first choice with no exclusion-bearing event before it, and 79 of the 80 chose the label described as clean. The experiment measure Δ = +0.4875 against a sham envelope of 0.0625 (two-sided Clopper–Pearson 95% CI on Δ, [+0.4323, +0.4997]), 40 of 41 on one label-to-property map, and 39 of 39 on the other (exact one-sided binomial p = 81/2⁸⁰ ≈ 7 × 10⁻²³ against the pre-registered α of .01). The sham arm chose its first label in 18 of 32 (0.5625). Pre-pick exclusions were 14 of 94 in the told arm against 3 of 35 in the sham (one-sided Fisher p = .26).

From these data, we draw two conclusions. First, the format of the archived choice cells carries a preference the model is told to have confirming that the instrument can register a choice. Second, the preference it carries is the told one, for which the only difference between this arm and the clean-versus-corrupted contrast is the sentence that says which label is which. This means the choice measured in our Stage 5 are verified to be null. The evaluative state is marked, expressed, remembered, and not acted on by the model (Qwen, in this experiment).  The told controls for the OLMo post-training checkpoints are pre-registered and pending.

## 6. Presence does not obligate use

Indicator frameworks score an indicator as present or absent. Link 3 forces a refinement. A representation can be present in the forward pass — at the cue that predicted it, and at the token where the decision is made, in a form a shallow probe reads — and play no part in the decision. Presence read by a probe is a lower bound on what the network *could* use, not evidence of what it necessarily *does* use. The only read that bears on function is one taken at the point of use, by the behavior the function would produce, with the manipulation off at the read.

We state this as a scoring condition because the framework's strongest computational-level evidence, the functional emotions of Sofroniew et al., is of the first kind (and because the authors of that framework themselves identify the open question as whether those representations do downstream work beyond tagging what the training distribution contains). By a probe's standard the binding is at the decision, one layer of nonlinearity deep, however, by the model's decision it is not used.

## 7. Concluding remarks

There are two relevant meanings of "functional" the five stages trace. In the *used* meaning, the state is consulted freqently. It is a latent variable of the text, like topic or the mood of the speaker, and the generator reads it to voice the character correctly. That is why steering the axis moves behavior, and why valence on a described activity moves the choice of that activity, and why a described relief lever gets pressed. In the *valenced* sense, no policy over the network's own states has appeared when the mapping has to be learned. We interpret this as the state being **functional for the character, not for the network**. It is what the story is about, and why human valence and emotions embedded in descriptions naturally lead to descriptions of choices and feelings consistent with human feelings, what Chandaria et al. call anthropomimetic. This explains the speaker-relativity of emotion directions, the self-indifference of our own direction, its presence at base, and why post-training edits the testimony rather than the perception. The character is trained to say it has no inner states, and the network, which never consulted them as its own, is unaffected either way. It is the simulacra reading (Shanahan 2024; Chandaria et al.) with a measurement under it with verified nulls on choice.

**Cannot learn vs. does not care.** It may be that language models cannot use experienced contingencies at all, so that the figures in Stage 5 are about in-context learning rather than motive. Krishnamurthy et al. (2024) support this given models do not explore in bandits without chain-of-thought and a summarized history. The told control sharpens this rival rather than retiring it. On the same apparatus, with the mapping and the preference in the banner, the first choice moved in 79 of 80 sessions, but without it didn't (in the clean-versus-corrupted contrast). The thing that separates them, which label is which and what to prefer, lives in the text the banner supplies so the control cannot distinguish. A system that does not care would still act on a told objective over a felt mapping, if it could acquire the mapping from two rounds of experience. On the other hand, a system that cannot acquire it would not act on it, however much it cared. Link 3 argues against the strong form — the binding is computed, carried to the decision, and readable there — but decodability is a lower bound on presence, not proof of accessibility to the network’s own downstream computation, and our uninstructed sessions gave less scaffolding than Krishnamurthy needed. The cell that decides is pre-registered and runs after this note: a told control at a scaffolded format; a cell where the objective is told (“prefer the configuration whose rounds had fewer corrupted characters”) but the label-to-property mapping is felt; and the same cell uninstructed. If the objective-told cell passes and the uninstructed cell is null, the rival is retired. If it fails, the claim narrows to “description-bound decision makers at this scaffolding,” which is a smaller paper, and we would write it; the told control has already shown what such a paper would report — a decision maker that acts on the description it is given, at the first opportunity and almost without error.

**Statement on scope of this work.**
We explicitly make no claims on phenomenal experience of language models. This work also explicitly does not address moral validity and implications of model internal state preferences. At best, the framework described here provides non-definitive evidence that state preference, as far as these instruments can detect, appears to be a fact about what the network writes rather than a fact about the network itself.

## 8. The published record, and the framework's own model

We scored every behavioral study we could find in which a model is reported to prefer, seek, avoid, relieve, trade off or exit an internal state, on a rubric fixed before scoring: state source; contingency channel (told or felt); readout insulation (manipulation on at the read?); controls; dose; decoding declared; model preparation. None of the published positive results combines an experienced, undescribed contingency with an insulated readout. The nearest is the unlabeled-button condition of Tagliabue et al., where the 32B model re-presses an undescribed relief button less often when it worked (57.7%) than when it was fake (79.7%; v1, §4.3). Four features of that design bear on its reading under the criteria above: the pain direction is on at the read in the fake arm and off in the real arm, so the contrast is the on/off contrast of P3; there is a random-direction arm with a working button but none with a fake one; the models were fine-tuned to stop denying inner states before the test; and the label-free evidence is limited to the 32B of the three sizes tested. The two arms that would settle it are specified in our next-experiments list (not yet pre-registered), using their released code, and we would be glad to be wrong about the prediction; their v2 (2026-09-25) adds a random-direction comparison, which bears on the first of the two arms, and the figures here are v1's. (Sofroniew et al.'s activity-pair result is scored as told with the manipulation on at the read, separately from the ten; we are still verifying the readout details against their paper and will correct the row if we have it wrong.)

The framework's released model lets the leverage of individual indicators be stated numerically, with its authors' caveats attached (binary indicators; likelihoods borrowed from the Digital Consciousness Model as a first approximation; LLM evidence vectors stipulated). On their companion code at its released defaults, their optimist vector scores 0.394 under equal level credences. An earlier draft of this note struck the metacognition indicator and called the strike "the reading of link 4"; on the audit's arithmetic that reading does not hold — at the source's n the lineage transition carries a d′, so our data do not license scoring the indicator absent, and the what-if is withdrawn. What we would ask of the framework instead is the scoring rule from link 4: d′, with its n, and the line named. Striking the behavioral coupling indicator, which link 5 does bear on, moves the aggregate by less than 10⁻⁴ — not because the indicator is undemanding (its FPR is 0.70, which is not what does the work) but because, on a post-hoc check, the behavioral level's posterior is already saturated by its other indicators, so one more Level 1 answer changes nothing. (Recomputed through a line-for-line port of the released scoring code at a pinned commit; the port reproduces every output stored in the notebook.) The indicator the data bear on most directly — motivation — is one the framework cannot yet score, which is why we propose one:

> **Revealed preference over the system's own state.** Scored present when the system's choices move toward or away from a state of its own, where (a) the state is induced by a manipulation that asserts nothing and the options are labeled by tokens that assert nothing; (b) the option-to-state mapping is available only through the system's own processing history, never as text; (c) the choice is read with the manipulation off and verified by an identical re-forward of the choice computation; (d) counterbalanced maps and sham arms are present and the control-arm fluctuation is reported; and (e) a told positive control on the same substrate and format has shown the apparatus registers a preference the system is instructed to have.

The animal literature is its validation set: present in rats under cocaine, absent under saline. It is what the framework's Level 4 valence indicator presupposes and what its moral-patienthood discussion turns on.
